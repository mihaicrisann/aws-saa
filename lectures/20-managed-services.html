Services you only need to recognise

The recognition half of the paper: a job described in plain English, and the one AWS service built for it. Free marks if the map is memorised.

Design High-Performing Architectures Design Secure Architectures Rekognition Textract Comprehend X-Ray Timestream Service Catalog

A slice of the exam is pure recognition. The scenario describes a job in plain English, three options are services that cannot do it, and one is the service built for exactly that. You do not need to know how these work — you need to map job → name in a couple of seconds and move on. This lecture is that map.

Why these questions are free marks

They carry the same weight as a VPC routing question and take a tenth of the time. They also have a tell: when an option would require you to build and train something yourself, and another option is a managed service that does it out of the box, the managed service wins — the qualifier is almost always "least operational overhead" or "fastest to implement".

The AI services, by input

Group them by what goes in and they stop blurring together.

The three pairs that get swapped

Textract vs Rekognition — a scanned document is Textract; a photograph or video frame is Rekognition.
Transcribe vs Polly — Transcribe listens, Polly speaks.
Kendra vs OpenSearch — Kendra answers a natural-language question over documents; OpenSearch is a search and log-analytics engine you operate and query.

Purpose-built databases

You know RDS, Aurora and DynamoDB. The rest appear as a single distractor-heavy question: the scenario names a data shape, and exactly one engine matches it.

Data shape → engine
Time-stamped metrics from devices, at scale→Timestream
Relationships, "friends of friends", fraud rings, recommendations→Neptune (graph)
Immutable, cryptographically verifiable ledger of changes→QLDB
Existing MongoDB application→DocumentDB
Existing Cassandra workload→Keyspaces
Petabyte-scale SQL analytics over a warehouse→Redshift
Ad-hoc SQL straight over files in S3→Athena
Ledger vs backup

QLDB questions describe an audit requirement — "prove no one altered the record". That is not the same as versioning or backups, which prove nothing about tampering. If the scenario says verifiable or immutable history of changes, it is QLDB.

Seeing inside a distributed application

Three tools, three different questions, and the exam distinguishes them sharply.

ToolAnswersCue
CloudWatch"Is it healthy, and what did it log?"Metrics, alarms, dashboards, log queries
CloudTrail"Who called which API, when?"Audit, governance, "who deleted it"
X-Ray"Where in the chain of services did the time go?"Tracing, "identify the bottleneck across microservices", "latency in one of the downstream calls"
One sentence that settles it

"Requests are slow but every service reports healthy, and we cannot tell which one is responsible" → X-Ray. Distributed tracing is the only thing that follows a single request across service boundaries.

Pinpoint is not SNS

SNS is application-to-application fan-out and operational alerts. Pinpoint is marketing to end users, with segments, campaigns and engagement analytics. "Notify the operations team" is SNS; "send a promotional SMS to customers who abandoned a basket" is Pinpoint.

Check yourself

Ten years of scanned paper contracts must become searchable structured data, with the parties and dates pulled out.
Textract to extract the text, forms and tables, then Comprehend to identify entities such as names and dates. Rekognition reads text in photographs, not document layouts, and SageMaker would mean building what these already do.
A microservices API is intermittently slow. Every service's CloudWatch alarms are green.
X-Ray. Enable tracing and read the service map — it attributes latency to a specific hop. Healthy per-service metrics are exactly the situation tracing exists for.
A fleet of sensors writes millions of time-stamped readings an hour, queried by time range.
Timestream. Purpose-built for time series, with automatic tiering from memory to cost-optimised storage and time-series functions built in. DynamoDB can store the data but you would build the retention and roll-ups yourself.
Developers must launch only approved, pre-configured stacks, without raw permissions.
Service Catalog. Administrators publish products with constraints; users launch from the menu with a launch role rather than their own permissions. Organizations and SCPs restrict what is possible but do not hand anyone a curated menu.

Take away

  1. Group the AI services by input: images (Rekognition), documents (Textract), audio in (Transcribe), audio out (Polly), text meaning (Comprehend), languages (Translate), document Q&A (Kendra), bots (Lex).
  2. SageMaker is only correct when nothing purpose-built fits or the scenario says custom model.
  3. Time series → Timestream. Graph → Neptune. Verifiable ledger → QLDB. MongoDB → DocumentDB. Cassandra → Keyspaces.
  4. CloudWatch = health and logs, CloudTrail = who called the API, X-Ray = where the latency is.
  5. Compute Optimizer right-sizes, Service Catalog hands out approved stacks, Personal Health Dashboard is about your resources.
  6. SNS notifies systems and operators; Pinpoint markets to customers.
